Primary Source Documents

Three primary-source documents form the financial backbone of the case. Two are creditor invoices issued to Hotshots Softball by Westinghouse Sportsplex (the practice field provider). The third is the reconstructed two-season financial spreadsheet showing inflows, claimed expenses, and the unpaid creditor balance.

2025 Two-Season Financial Reconciliation
Firsthand Type: Working spreadsheet Periods: 2/10/25–8/1/25 + 8/18/25–12/17/25

What this shows. A reconstructed two-season ledger covering the full 2025 calendar. Player dues totaled $18,150 ($150 × 11 players × 11 months). Fundraising totaled $6,250 (February football pool, February BBQ, Hotshots-hosted tournament). Combined inflows: $24,400.

Claimed expenses split between tournaments ($9,000) and practice field costs ($9,000) for an estimated total of $18,000. The critical line: “25 GATEWAY UNPAID INVOICES: $9,000” (highlighted red on the spreadsheet) indicates the practice field expenses were never actually paid to the field provider. Actual costs paid: $9,000. MC Net Profit Estimate: $15,400.

Combined with the Spring invoice above, total outstanding to Westinghouse Sportsplex is $9,000. Both invoices are dated and itemized; both are unpaid.

Why these three documents matter together
The reconciliation spreadsheet identifies the structural problem — $24,400 collected, only $9,000 actually paid out, $15,400 net retained by Mark. The two Westinghouse invoices are the receipts. They prove an identifiable third party is owed money for services that families already paid for through dues. This is no longer just a 501(c)(3) compliance question; it is a creditor-obligation question with a documented counterparty.
