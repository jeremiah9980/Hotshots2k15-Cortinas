Filing Routes

How to Report — Regulator Routing

Each finding maps to a specific regulator with jurisdiction. Multiple agencies can be filed with in parallel; none of them coordinate, so simultaneous filings increase the likelihood of action.

IRS — Form 13909 (Tax-Exempt Organization Complaint)

Covers
Private inurement, private benefit, alter ego operation, filing-tier abuse, NTEE misclassification, Form 1023 disclosure issues, co-mingling of charitable funds.
Form
Form 13909 (PDF)
Submit to
EOClass@irs.gov — or by mail to IRS EO Examinations, 1100 Commerce St., Mail Code 4900 DAL, Dallas, TX 75242
Anonymous?
Yes. Identifying yourself helps with follow-up but is not required.
What to attach
Screenshots of ahctx.org, the phone reverse-lookup result, the Zeffy listing, the BBQ fundraiser post, and a written narrative summary. The Executive Summary page on this portal is filing-ready.
Outcome
IRS reviews and may open an examination. Resolution can include revocation of tax-exempt status, retroactively if warranted, plus assessment of excise taxes on insiders.

Texas Attorney General — Charitable Trust Section

Covers
Private inurement, private benefit, alter ego, governance failures, co-mingling. The Texas AG has authority to demand records the org will not produce voluntarily.
How to file
texasattorneygeneral.gov — consumer complaint portal, choose “charitable organization” category.
Phone
(800) 252-8011
Anonymous?
Identifying yourself is generally required for the AG to follow up; a sworn complaint carries more weight.
Outcome
The AG can investigate, demand records, seek injunctive relief, remove officers, and dissolve the entity. They typically act when there is a clear public interest in protecting donors and beneficiaries.

Texas Comptroller — Exempt Organizations Section

Covers
State sales and franchise tax exemption review. Different regulator than the AG; reviews whether the org meets Texas-specific exemption tests independent of federal status.
Contact
exempt.orgs@cpa.texas.gov · (800) 252-5555
Outcome
Can revoke state-level exemption independent of any IRS action.

Texas Secretary of State

Covers
Verification of nonprofit corporation registration, registered agent, and certificate-of-formation accuracy. This is informational, not a complaint route, but the records pulled here support all other filings.
Access
SOSDirect — ~$1 per record, immediate online access
Phone
(512) 463-5555

IRS Form 4506-A — Request for Public Inspection

Covers
Obtaining the original Form 1023 application and all 990-N filings for Active Horizons. These are public records by law; the org cannot refuse.
Form
Form 4506-A (PDF)
Cost
Free for individual requesters of a single org's filings.
Turnaround
Several weeks.
Why this matters
Form 1023 is the document where the org told the IRS who controls it and what relationships exist among insiders. If those disclosures don't match reality, that is independently actionable.

Recommended Filing Order

  1. Pull SOS & Comptroller records first. $1 each, available immediately. Locks in who is publicly on the hook.
  2. Submit Form 4506-A to the IRS. Triggers nothing, but starts the clock on getting the Form 1023 in hand.
  3. File IRS Form 13909 and Texas AG complaint in parallel. Different agencies, different jurisdictions, no coordination — both should be filed simultaneously to maximize the likelihood of action.
  4. Notify Hotshots organization leadership. If the for-profit brand has any oversight structure, they may have an interest in distancing themselves from a member team commingling brand and 501(c)(3).
  5. Consult counsel before any direct demand to the org. Anything you say to Mark Cortinas or his family directly becomes evidence in either direction; route formal requests through the regulators above.
Note on Anonymity
IRS Form 13909 can be filed anonymously. Texas AG complaints generally cannot be effective anonymously. SOS and Comptroller record pulls are anonymous by default. Decide your preferred posture before filing — once filed, you cannot unfile.
