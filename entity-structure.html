Why NTEE O12 Matters

The IRS uses NTEE codes to classify the function of every tax-exempt organization. The code is self-selected by the org during the Form 1023 application and reviewed by the IRS.

  • N60–N69 — Recreation, Sports, Leisure, Athletics. Where a youth sports development charity belongs.
  • O12 — Fund Raising and/or Fund Distribution. The code Active Horizons selected (or accepted).

The classification matters because it confirms in IRS records what the organization tells the government it does. By selecting O12, the entity is registered as a money-moving vehicle — not as an athletic development program. That is inconsistent with the public-facing “Youth Sports Initiative” branding and with the 501(c)(3) educational/charitable purpose required for youth sports orgs.

The 990-N Filing Tier

Form 990-N is a postcard. An organization that files only the 990-N discloses nothing publicly except its name, EIN, address, and an attestation that gross receipts are under $50,000 annually.

What 990-N does NOT disclose:

  • Income statement
  • Expenses
  • Officer or director compensation
  • Program descriptions
  • Balance sheet
  • Related-party transactions (which Schedule L of the full 990 would require)

This means the inability to show records to families is not just stonewalling — it is structural. By selecting the 990-N filing tier, the organization has ensured there are no detailed public records to produce, by design.

If actual annual gross receipts exceed $50K (which one team's reported $16,200 across six months strongly suggests is plausible across multiple teams and tournaments), then the 990-N selection is itself a filing violation. See Regulatory Actions.
