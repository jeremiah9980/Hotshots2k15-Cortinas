The Three Money Rails

Family payments to a single team are reportedly accepted across three legally distinct destinations:

Rail 1: For-profit Hotshots Brand fees, gear markup, organization dues
+
Rail 2: Active Horizons 501(c)(3) Tournament fundraising via Zeffy; tax-deductible donations
+
Rail 3: Personal Chase Venmo-linked, in coach's name

Each rail has different legal requirements, different tax treatment, and different accountability standards. Co-mingling across rails — especially mixing charitable funds with personal accounts — is a fundamental fiduciary violation regardless of intent.

The Timing Tell

May dues collected as normal → the assistant coach asking about the money is removed → the rest of the season is suddenly “free.”

That sequence does two things at once. It looks like retaliation against the person asking inconvenient questions, and it tacitly admits there was always enough money for the season to be free. If the program were genuinely running tight, you cannot flip a switch and waive months of dues. The switch existing means the money was there — it just was not being applied to offset family costs while the questions were being asked from inside.

Material Risk Signal

Reconciliation Gap
Fundraising alone (~$11,250) was sufficient to cover the entire season's team-level expenses (~$8,700). Despite this, families contributed an additional ~$4,950 in dues with no documented allocation. The unaccounted balance of ~$7,500 represents a material transparency gap that has not been reconciled across any of the three money rails.
